I study how new genes are born. Working across population genetics, single-cell and spatial-omics, biophysics, and evolutionary theory, I ask how “junk” DNA evolves into functional genes de novo, and how genome organization, transcriptional noise, and molecular structure shape that process.

Bio

UnJin Lee is an evolutionary geneticist with a background in physics. His earlier work centered on the interface between cancer diagnostics and systems biology, emphasizing statistical and numerical methods. As a graduate student in the Committee on Genetics, Genomics, and Systems Biology at the University of Chicago, he studied the functional evolution of new, essential genes and the evolution of phenotypic plasticity. He is currently an NSF Postdoctoral Fellow in the Laboratory of Evolutionary Genetics and Genomics at the Rockefeller University, where he studies the selective forces that drive non-coding regions of the genome to evolve into new de novo genes.
